Why we are campaigning

The closure isn't affecting just one road or one neighbourhood

Albert Bridge has been closed since February 2026, creating increased congestion, longer journeys and significant disruption on both sides of the river.

It is having a knock-on effect across Battersea, Chelsea and the surrounding roads, with increased traffic, longer journeys and disruption for residents, businesses, families and visitors. Albert Bridge is an important connection between our communities.

We understand that the bridge needs to be properly repaired and made safe. Our campaign is not against the necessary repairs — it is about how the closure is being managed, and how long it takes.

We are calling for

  1. Urgent coordinated action
  2. Greater transparency about progress
  3. Clear information and timelines
  4. Effective management of the wider traffic impacts
  5. Every effort to minimise the length of the closure

Next steps with the Deputy Mayor

We are working on a follow-up with Seb Dance, Deputy Mayor of London for Transport.

RBKC

Meeting with the bridge's owner

We are meeting Andrew Burton, Director of Transport & Regulatory Services at RBKC — a key point of contact for Albert Bridge — to push for answers, progress and greater clarity.

Media

Local and national press

We are reaching out to journalists and local media to highlight the impact the closure is having on Battersea and Chelsea.

Air quality

Why motorists matter

Traffic is now gridlocked on both sides of the river, pushing up air pollution in residential streets on both banks. The bridge needs to reopen to motorists — for our health, not just our journey times.
